Measurement rules

The first endpoint is the earlier Knoxville low and the second endpoint is the newer lower low. The line length is reported in trading bars and calendar days. The price drop is (first low - second low) / first low.

The trade backtest buys at the next session's high and sets its sell target the same percentage above entry as the drop between the two Knoxville endpoints. It exits at that target on the first later session whose high reaches it. Positions that never reach the target remain open rather than being counted as wins.

The equal-bounce target adds that same percentage to the second low. Full recovery instead returns to the first low; these differ because a fall requires a larger percentage gain to recover completely. Recovery checks start on the next session, never on the signal candle.

High recovery means the intraday high touched the target. Close recovery requires the daily close to reach it. Medians use recovered events only, so recovery rates and unrecovered counts remain visible beside them.

Likely corporate-action discontinuities are retained in the complete event CSV with a reason, but excluded from qualified rates and current candidates.

Important: this is an event study of today's Kite-listed NSE universe, so delisted stocks are absent and survivorship bias remains. It is not a portfolio return or a promise of future performance.
